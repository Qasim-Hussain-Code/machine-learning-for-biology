Machine Learning for Biology | Day 46

Four chapters. Four models. Here is where we have been.

Chapter 1 was logistic regression, the simplest useful model, a straight line drawn through a decision.

Chapter 2 was a single decision tree, a model you can read out loud, one split at a time.

Chapter 3 was a random forest, many trees voting together, each one blind to what the others saw.

Chapter 4 was gradient boosting, trees built one after another, each one correcting what the last one got wrong, adapted to handle patients whose outcome had not happened yet.

That is the standard path through tree-based models, the one most textbooks follow in roughly the same order. It ends here.

Chapter 5 moves to support vector machines, the next step on that same standard progression.

What I am not disclosing yet is the question.

Every chapter so far has paired one model with one biological question chosen to make that model earn its place. I have an idea for what that question should be this time. I am not posting it today.

Instead, I am asking first.

If you have a biological question you think support vector machines are actually suited to, one that a simpler model in this series could not have handled honestly, you can tell me. 

I will read what comes in. Tomorrow I will name the actual question this chapter builds around, and Chapter 5 starts from there.
